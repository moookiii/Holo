# Favorites binder

Favorites uses a physical Three.js binder in the existing gallery session. Each
face has four columns and three rows, matching the user's reference and explicit
layout choice. Each binder always contains 20 double-sided sheets, 40 faces, and
480 positions, even when empty. Further favorites allocate additional binders.

The default opening is the first interior spread (faces 2–3, 24 positions). Face 1
is reached by turning the left sheet back toward the inside cover. The last opening
contains face 40 and the rear inside cover. This preserves sequential physical
face ordering; the first 12 favorites are on face 1.

## Construction

The rounded shell has thickness, compressed padding, woven nylon maps, leather
micrograin, a rounded zipper tape, two rows of individual metal teeth, perimeter
stitches, and a modeled slider/pull with an open centre. All 20 sheet separators
and transparent edge rims remain represented with inexpensive curved geometry. Stack height
moves from right to left as sheets turn. Thin-sheet bevels are limited by thickness.

Clear sleeve films have raised entry lips, edge wrinkles, separate PVC normals,
angle-dependent opacity, and clearcoat reflection. Double weld channels and small
annular recesses model the perforated seam texture between sleeves. The recesses
have open centres exposing the dark separator, rather than painted bright dots.
The frame stays fixed throughout turns. Hover never deforms a sheet. Binder-only PNG maps are generated by `scripts/binder/material-textures.py`.
These procedural binder materials are an approximation of the reference photograph,
not measured fabric/PVC scans.

## Navigation and rendering

Press anywhere on a page and drag to turn it. Plain card clicks inspect the card. Pointer capture
keeps the sheet attached during a drag. Crossing the threshold completes the turn;
a short drag or cancellation returns it. A damped spring settles the sheet. Both
faces move together, with bending concentrated between rigid cards. Arrow buttons
and keyboard arrows offer secondary navigation. Dragging that starts outside all binder geometry tilts the binder
within restrained bounds; wheel input does not change framing. The default view is nearly straight-on.

Every occupied pocket uses Holo's existing CardInstance and CardFactory. Card fronts,
foil, authored etching, masks, profiles, geometry and viewer transitions are unchanged.
Only the current spread and its immediate neighbors can hold expensive live cards:
at most six faces / 72 cards, independent of the collection size. Serial preparation
prioritizes the current spread, then neighbors. Departed faces dispose their cards.
Inspection stops preparation before entering the full-quality viewer. Returning to
Favorites restores the same binder and spread. Favorites storage and canonical IDs
continue to use the existing gallery implementation.

## Verification

`tests/binder-layout.test.ts` checks complete unique coverage through 10,000 favorites,
480/481 and 960/961 boundaries, the initial interior spread, every physical page turn
forward/backward, cancellation and drag thresholds, and deformation registration.

`scripts/binder-check.mjs` drives actual Chromium pointer input, checks incomplete and
complete drags, walks every page through the back cover and back to the front, checks
populated and empty later pages, selects a third binder, and opens/returns from the
full card viewer. Captures and results are written to `artifacts/favorites-binder/`.
The browser check uses the WebGL fallback by default; set BINDER_URL for another
running backend. Screenshots support visual review, not a claim of measured physical
material accuracy or a guarantee of frame rate on every GPU.

## Motion scheduling

Dense film and seam vertices remain static. A 513-sample float texture carries the
sheet's position/tangent curve into the vertex shader; the authored film normal maps
remain active. Surfaces are merged into three material draws per face. Raycasting
uses a separate 96-by-4 separator, and cards remain rigid instances following the
same tangents. This removes dense vertex uploads and normal/bounds reconstruction
from each turn frame. Hidden stationary pages do not repeat deformation work.

GPU card realization, per-texture uploads, compilation and attachment wait while a
page turn or orbit drag is active. Already-started GPU operations can finish, but
subsequent loading steps yield until motion settles. The pause callback is optional
in CardFactory, so normal viewer/gallery uploads retain their existing behavior.
